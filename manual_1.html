Covers every system on the floor: Control Panel · Zanna Clock backend v1.11.0 and kiosk v1.2 · Payroll app v1.0 · Data Maintenance console 1.4.0 / server 1.3.0 · FairLeave v2.10 · Shift Scheduler · Time and Motion

Not in here on purpose: PINs, where PINs are kept, and the addresses of the management screens. This page sits in a public repository, so anything written on it is public no matter what the browser is told. Managers: that detail is held separately — ask.

Break pay is controlled by the Exclude Time column instead — Part 5. Following the old guide will have you changing a value and wondering why nothing happens.

Apps and spreadsheets

The apps are only screens. All of the actual data lives in three spreadsheets, and each one owns something different:

Spreadsheet Owns
Shift Scheduler People. Who exists, what days they work
Zanna Clock Time. Every clock-in, every break, every paid hour
FairLeave Holidays. Entitlement, bookings, swaps

One rule prevents most of the confusion in this manual: fix a thing where it is owned. Correcting somebody's name in the Clock when the Scheduler owns names looks like it worked, and is quietly undone at 05:00 the next morning.

PART 2 — For staff

Print this page for the staff room. Nothing else here concerns them.

Clocking in

  1. Tap your name
  2. Tap your four digits

That's it. The screen says ✅ Clocked In, goes back to the list, and your name turns green. No other buttons.

The very first time, you'll be asked to choose a PIN instead of enter one. Pick four digits you'll remember. It's yours from then on.

Breaks and going home

Tap your name, enter your PIN, then choose:

  • ☕ 15 minute break or 🍽 30 minute break — you'll be asked whether you're staying on site or leaving the building. Answer honestly: in a fire, the register is what tells the marshal whether to look for you.
  • 🚻 Comfort break — not timed, doesn't affect your pay
  • ✅ End my break when you come back
  • 🔴 Clock OUT at the end of your shift

What the colours mean

On the list Means
Plain white Not clocked in
Green with a ✓ On shift
Amber On a break

If something goes wrong

  • Your name isn't there — see a manager. Don't just start work; the hours won't be recorded.
  • It won't take your PIN — check you're on your own name. Three wrong tries is a good moment to ask for a reset rather than a fourth.
  • You forgot to clock out yesterday — tell a manager. It's fixable, but only if someone knows.

Forgetting to clock out is the most common problem, and it inflates your hours until it's corrected. Nobody gets in trouble for owning up on the day.

PART 3 — How the whole thing fits together

Three spreadsheets. One rule explains most confusion:

        Shift Scheduler   ← owns PEOPLE
          ↙          ↘
    FairLeave        Zanna Clock
   (holidays)        (clocking, payroll)

Staff are never added in the Clock. They're added in the Shift Scheduler, and two systems copy from it independently, at different times, from different screens. Update one and the other is unchanged.

PART 4 — Everyday jobs

Adding a new starter

Do this in the app, not in the spreadsheet.

  1. Data Maintenance → Employees → “+ Add employee”. Fill in the name and whatever else you have, leave Active as yes, and Save. One button does two things: it writes the person into the Shift Scheduler — which is still the source of truth — and then runs FairLeave's import for you, so they get a leave record and a personal link
  2. Data Maintenance → Employees → Sync to Clock — puts them on the kiosk. This one is not automatic on save
  3. Check the kiosk. Their name must be visible before their first shift

Step 2 also runs by itself at 05:00, so someone starting tomorrow is covered either way — but if they start today, press the button.

They set their own PIN at first use. You never issue one.

What the form does not cover. The Add employee form handles identity, team, leave and clocking. It does not set a working pattern. If the new person doesn't work a standard week — fewer contracted days, no Saturdays — open the Shift Scheduler afterwards and set ContractedDays, NoSaturday and their row on EmployeePatterns by hand. Skip that and the rota will schedule them for days they don't work, and leave will be charged against days they were never due in.

Editing the Scheduler's Employees tab directly still works — it's the same sheet the form writes to. But then steps 1 and 2 are both on you: Sync from Scheduler, then Sync to Clock. The form exists so that can't be forgotten.

Someone leaves

Data Maintenance → Employees → open their row → Archive. That marks them inactive in the Shift Scheduler, re-syncs FairLeave, cancels their approved bookings and fails any pending swaps. Then press Sync to Clock to drop them off the kiosk.

The equivalent by hand is Active = FALSE in the Shift Scheduler followed by Sync to Clock — same result on the kiosk, but their outstanding leave and swaps are left dangling.

Either way their history is untouched.

Never delete them from the Clock's Staff tab. Every historical row refers to them by name, and their PIN hash lives on that row.

Someone forgets their PIN

Data Maintenance → Employees → open their row → Reset kiosk PIN.

Next time they tap their name at the kiosk they're asked to choose a new one. There is nothing to issue and nothing to write down — you never see anyone's PIN, including your own staff's. Their clocking history is untouched.

By hand, if the console can't reach the Clock: open the Zanna Clock spreadsheet, Staff tab, find their row and clear both PinHash and Salt.

Clear both or neither. A hash with no salt can never match, and they'd be locked out permanently with no way to enrol again.

Someone is off sick

Menu → 🤒 Record sick absence. They then show as absent on the canteen and office displays and in the emergency register.

Someone forgot to clock out

Open Events, find their CLOCK_IN, and add a CLOCK_OUT row with the right time, the same SessionID, and the same name. Then rebuild.

Editing the log is a real decision, not a formality. It changes what someone is paid, and the Audit tab won't show your reasoning — put it in the Device column or tell payroll.

PART 5 — Break rules, and how pay is actually calculated

One setting decides everything: the Exclude Time column on the Config tab.

Any Config row whose key starts with BREAK_ is a break type. The value is the allowed minutes. Two columns then decide how it behaves:

Column Question it answers
D — Comfort Break Yes = untimed, always on site, never shows a countdown
E — Exclude Time YES = these minutes are deducted from pay. Anything else = paid

Current settings:

Key Mins Comfort Exclude Time Effect
BREAK_15 15 YES deducted
BREAK_30 30 YES deducted
BREAK_COMFORT 0 Yes NO paid, untimed

So someone on an 8-hour shift taking both breaks is paid 7 hours 15 minutes.

Check it, never assume it: Menu → 🩺 Show break pay rules.

Two things worth knowing:

  • Column D is not column E. They're adjacent and they answer different questions. Reading one when you meant the other is a bug that once made payroll deduct nothing at all for weeks.
  • A break type missing from Config is treated as PAID and flagged, never silently deducted. The Payroll app shows those rows in amber. The Clock will not quietly reduce someone's pay based on a rule nobody wrote down.

Adding a break type

Add a Config row: key BREAK_SOMETHING, value = minutes, Notes = the button label staff see, and YES in Exclude Time if it's unpaid. It appears on the kiosk immediately — the buttons are built from Config, not hard-coded.

PART 6 — Running payroll

Control Panel → Payroll. You should not need to open the spreadsheet.

  1. Sign in with the management PIN (the Clock's — Part 8)
  2. Process payroll — rebuilds Sessions and Payroll from the raw log. Safe to run as often as you like; it never changes what anyone clocked
  3. Read the summary. Open shifts, unclosed breaks and unconfigured break types are reported here, not buried
  4. Pick the week, check the totals, Export to Excel

Reading the results

Column Meaning
Gross minutes Total time between clock-in and clock-out
Breaks deducted Break types marked Exclude Time = YES
Breaks paid Everything else
Net paid minutes Gross − deducted

Amber rows need checking before you pay them. They contain a break type that isn't in Config, so those minutes were treated as paid.

"Still open" means someone never clocked out. Their shift contributes nothing until it's corrected — see Part 4.

One trap worth knowing

There is an older export at ?action=payroll in a URL. It uses a different calculation: first clock-in to last clock-out of the day, which pays people for the gap between a split shift, and it predates the per-break-type rules.

Use the Payroll app. If someone hands you a CSV from that link, the numbers may not match and the app is the one that's right.

Each device has a row on the Devices tab with a token, and that token is in its URL. Only a KIOSK device can clock anyone in — the displays are read-only even if someone finds the URL.

To retire a tablet: set its Devices row to Active = FALSE. The URL stops working immediately.

The emergency register is a safety document. It's only as good as the on-site / off-site answers staff give when starting a break — which is why the kiosk asks.

PART 8 — Management access

The management PIN

The Payroll app, the Data Maintenance console and FairLeave's management screens are each behind a PIN. Staff never need one — everything in Part 2 works without.

The PINs are not written down here. They are not kept in any spreadsheet either, which is deliberate: a spreadsheet cell is readable by everyone who can edit the rota. If you need one, ask — do not go looking for it in the sheets, and do not paste one into a message or a document.

Each system has its own PIN, and some of them are paired across systems. Changing one on its own is the single most common way to break syncing between two systems, and the error it produces mentions permissions, which sends people looking in entirely the wrong place. Never change a PIN without checking the internal rotation notes first.

If management gets locked out

Ten wrong attempts locks management out for 15 minutes. The counter is global, not per-person — so one person guessing locks it for everybody, including someone who knows the right PIN. Wait it out, or ask for it to be cleared.

Staff clocking in and out is completely unaffected. The kiosk keeps working through a management lockout.

The two traps that cost the most time

  1. Saving is not deploying. The web app serves the last deployed version. The symptom is a screen calling something the server has never heard of.
  2. Every .gs file in a project shares one namespace, and a duplicated function name is resolved by taking the last one — with no warning. A correct fix once did nothing for three rounds because a second file redefined it. 🩺 Which code is live exists precisely for this.

PART 12 — FairLeave: booking holidays

There is no approval step. Nobody signs your holiday off. If there is room on the days you want, they are yours the moment you ask. First come, first served.

Booking a holiday

Open your personal link, pick your dates, submit. It is approved instantly unless one of three things is true:

  • the dates fall in a blackout period the business has closed off
  • you do not have enough days left
  • your team is already at its limit on one of those days

When your dates are already taken

You are not simply refused. You are shown who holds the days you wanted, and you can ask them to swap.

A swap is a true exchange, not a favour. The other person accepts by choosing their own replacement dates, and both moves are made together — or neither is. Nobody ends up having given up their holiday and got nothing back.

If they would rather just release the days without rebooking, that works too.

What Saturdays cost you

Saturday is a working day here, so it counts towards cover — it affects how many people can be off at once.

But it does not come off your entitlement. A Monday-to-Friday week off costs you five days, not six, so 20 days is exactly four working weeks. Sundays are the same. This was corrected in August 2026 and applies to leave already booked, not just new bookings.

Your personal link

Your link signs you in as you. Anyone who has it can change your bookings. Treat it like your bank card, not like a bookmark — do not forward it, do not put it in a group chat, do not leave it open on a shared tablet.

Add it to your phone's home screen and it behaves like an app. There is also a QR code you can scan instead of typing it.

Lost it? Ask a manager to reissue. You get a new link and the old one stops working, which is the point.

What managers set

Management does not approve individual requests — it sets the limits the rules run on: how many people may be off at once in each team, which periods are blacked out, and how many days each person gets.

Check the teams before setting a limit. A limit of two means two people off across whatever the team covers. If everybody is in one team, that is two people off in the entire business.

How it connects to everything else

People come from the Shift Scheduler. Approved leave is pushed back to the Scheduler so the rota knows. The Clock reads FairLeave, so anyone on holiday shows as on holiday on the displays rather than as simply absent.

If a booking fails to reach the Scheduler, the holiday is still booked — the sync is allowed to fail without taking somebody's leave down with it.

PART 13 — The Shift Scheduler

Not an app — a spreadsheet. It is also the most important thing in the estate, because it owns people.

If somebody is not marked Active here, they do not exist anywhere else. Not on the kiosk, not in FairLeave, not in payroll. Every other system takes its copy from this one.

What lives only here

The Add employee form in Data Maintenance covers identity, team, leave and clocking. It does not set a working pattern. Contracted days, whether someone works Saturdays, and individual weekly patterns exist only in the Scheduler.

Skip them and two things go wrong quietly: the rota schedules people for days they do not work, and leave is charged against days they were never due in.

Each system takes its own copy

The Clock and FairLeave both copy from the Scheduler independently, at different times, from different screens. Syncing one does not sync the other. This is the single most common source of "but I already fixed that".

Fix the person here, then push the change out — Part 4 has the buttons.

Never fix a person on the Clock's own Staff tab. It looks like it worked. The 05:00 sync overwrites it from the Scheduler and the problem is back before anyone is in.

What else it feeds

Once somebody is Active and clocking, the Scheduler's data drives the reporting: production timing, absence patterns, how many staff are rostered against what the week actually needs, and the payroll export.

PART 14 — Time and Motion

It is part of the Shift Scheduler, not a separate system. There is no separate login and nothing to install.

What it measures

The Clock measures attendance — who was here and for how long. Time and Motion measures work — how long production tasks actually take. Two different questions, and the second one is the one that tells you whether a shift was well spent.

Its actual status

Built in May 2026. Never implemented. It was offered to Zanna that June alongside three other systems, a date was going to be set, and it never was. It has been sitting finished and unused since.

There is a second reason it cannot be used as it stands: access is restricted to Richmond IT accounts, so a Zanna login will not open it. That is a setting rather than a fault, but it has to be decided before any rollout — otherwise the first person who tries will report a broken link, when what they actually hit was a permission boundary.

Nothing depends on it. No other system reads from it, and nothing breaks while it sits idle. It needs a decision, not a repair.

PART 15 — Built, but not systems

Things that exist, look finished, and are not part of the running estate. Listed so nobody spends an afternoon hunting for a login that was never made.

Factory Floor Planner

A drag-and-drop allocation board — sections down the side, shifts across the top, staff dragged into the cells from a pool. It also tracks certification status, which nothing else here does.

It is a prototype. There is no backend, it is connected to none of the systems above, and it saves nothing — refresh the page and the board is gone. The names in it are invented.

Not deployed, no address, no login. Worth keeping for the certification idea; not worth looking for.

PART 16 — What changed since the v1.2 guide

Then Now
PAY_BREAKS with three modes Gone. Exclude Time per break type
One shift row per person per day One per shift — split shifts count separately
Staff matched by name Matched by EmployeeID, so a rename keeps the PIN
Management PIN in Config Out of the spreadsheet entirely, plus a lockout after repeated wrong attempts
Clock in = tap, PIN, then Clock In One tap — PIN verified and clocked in together
Payroll by copy-paste from the tab The Payroll app, with an Excel export
Breaks not in Config ignored Treated as paid and flagged

Two fixes worth knowing about because they silently affected pay:

  • Breaks taken on the kiosk never reached payroll. The end-of-break row didn't record which break it ended, so nothing paired. Fixed in v1.3.1.
  • A second shift in one day overwrote the first. Both used the same SessionID, so the hours vanished. Fixed in v1.5, and old collisions are recovered on rebuild.
